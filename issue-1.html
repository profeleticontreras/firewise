We Are All Protecting Our Community

Hello neighbors, and welcome to the first Firewise Corner, where we share practical ways to keep our neighborhood safe from wildfire. I am Leti, your Firewise chair. Embers and fire cross property lines, so each yard you prepare protects the yards around it.

Part of my role as chair is sharing information like this newsletter, gathering information about the work our neighbors do, and submitting our Firewise USA renewal application.

November is renewal application month, so I am asking for your help to paint an accurate picture of what our neighbors are doing to keep everyone safe. Every hour counts, and estimates are fine. Pruning, clearing gutters, moving firewood, and hauling green waste all qualify, whether you did it alone or with others.

Know Your 3 Zones

ZoneDistanceWhat to do
Zone 00 to 5 ftGravel, stone, pavers, or brick. No wood mulch, bark, or dense plants. Nothing stored against walls, windows, or decks.
Zone 15 to 30 ftRemove dead plants, dry grass, leaves, and needles. Space plants apart. Grass 4 inches or shorter.
Zone 230 to 100 ftTrim annual grass to 4 inches. Space shrubs and trees apart; about 10 ft between trees on flat to mild slopes.

Zone 0 update: California adopted emergency 5-foot ember-resistant zone rules on August 19, 2026, and effective dates are still being confirmed. Board of Forestry Zone 0 page

6 Big Wins for This Weekend

  1. Clear gutters and the roof of leaves and pine needles.
  2. Move wood piles and propane tanks away from the home.
  3. Keep patio furniture and swing sets away from trees and shrubs.
  4. Trim branches over the roof, 10 ft from roofs and chimneys.
  5. Prune lower tree branches to 6 ft above the ground.
  6. Replace wood mulch within 5 ft of the home with gravel or stone.
